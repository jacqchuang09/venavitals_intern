    The science

    Applanation tonometry,
    made soft.

    The sensor reads the artery’s pulse right through the skin. Each beat pushes up into a soft capacitor.

    Sensing principle

    A soft parallel-plate capacitor.

    C = ε₀ εᵣ Ad

    When the artery swells, the gap d between the gold plates shrinks and air (εᵣ = 1) is pushed out, so capacitance goes up. Calibration turns that into mmHg.

    100%gap d vs. at rest
    1.00×capacitance vs. at rest

    The gap change is exaggerated so it’s visible.

    Sensing mechanism

    Soft enough to wear. Accurate enough to trust.

    The electrodes are wrinkled gold (wAu) thin film. The micro-wrinkles let the gold sit on soft silicone, so the sensor bends and stretches with the skin instead of cracking.

      • Electrodes reach 200% strain before electrical failure
      • The wrinkles unfold like an accordion instead of cracking
      • Molded ~85 µm micro-ridges hold the top electrode over an air cavity
      • Air compresses more than silicone, so each beat moves the plate further
      • About 5× the ridge-free design over 0–10 kPa
      • 5,000 load cycles at 25 kPa without failure
      • Wrinkled structure tolerates repeated bending, flexing and stretching
      • Soft, skin-safe materials conform to the body for a better sensor-to-skin interface
      • Streams to a bedside iPad monitor
      • Nothing tethered to the patient or crossing the surgical field

    Figures: Kim et al., Adv. Healthcare Mater., 2019.

    Lessons learned

    Design for failure modes.

    01

    Hardware

    Assume the connection fails.

    Failure

    A cable pulls or the sensor is overloaded mid-test.

    Design

    Force and travel limits, disconnect detection, auto-recovery.

     02

    Software

    Keep the change small.

    Failure

    A fix ripples into code that was already validated.

    Design

    The QC fix touched only the display layer, so nothing else needed retesting.

    Process

    Prove the check can fail.
